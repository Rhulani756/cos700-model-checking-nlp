Focus on the pointer that is dereferenced without being proven valid.

When the counterexample contains enough evidence, explain:
- the pointer expression involved, such as *ptr, ptr[index], or ptr->member;
- where the pointer is initialized, assigned, returned, or left null;
- the branch, input, failed allocation, or failed lookup that permits the null value to reach the dereference;
- the exact source location and pointer value shown by the trace;
- the validity condition that should hold before the dereference.

Distinguish the immediate failure from the earlier logical cause. A null check immediately before the dereference may prevent the crash, but it may not correct the path that incorrectly produced or propagated the null pointer.

Do not claim that a pointer is null unless the source or trace shows it. Do not invent ownership, lifetime, allocation, or initialization facts. Suggest deliberate initialization, explicit handling of failure paths, and a validity check only when those changes fit the supplied evidence.
